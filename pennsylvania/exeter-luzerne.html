Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

When you renovate a kitchen in Exeter, Pennsylvania, the permits and licensing rules usually matter more than homeowners expect—especially if you move plumbing fixtures or upgrade electrical. In Pennsylvania, contractors performing home improvement work over $500 generally must be properly registered under the Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) framework (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). For kitchen electrical work, any circuit installation or modification must be performed by (or under the supervision of) a licensed Pennsylvania electrician—verify credentials through dli.pa.gov.

Plumbing work (such as changing a sink location, adding a dishwasher hookup, or rerouting supply/drain lines) must be done by or under the supervision of a PA-licensed plumber. Most municipalities in PA require building permits and inspections for kitchen renovations involving plumbing rerouting, new electrical circuits, or wall removal. Cosmetic work—like painting, installing new hardware, or cabinet refacing without disturbing the box structure—typically does not trigger the same permit path.

Because many Exeter-area homes were built before 1978, lead-based paint may exist on kitchen surfaces. If contractors disturb lead paint, EPA Lead-Safe RRP certification is required; verify certifications at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov before a gut remodel in older homes starts.

Finally, the PA Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires written contracts for all home improvement work over $500, including a 3-day right of cancellation—so insist on a signed scope and payment terms in writing.

Step-by-step verification in Exeter: (1) confirm the contractor’s HIC registration online; (2) ask for a COI (certificate of insurance) showing general liability, and request workers’ compensation proof if required by law; (3) confirm electrician and plumber licenses for any electrical or plumbing scope; and (4) obtain written confirmation that permits/inspections will be handled before demolition or rough-ins.

Cabinet refacing vs. replacement — what makes sense in Exeter?

With about 69.4% of Exeter’s housing stock built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many Luzerne County homeowners have kitchens where the cabinet boxes are still structurally usable but the style is dated. Cabinet refacing—replacing door and drawer fronts and applying veneer while keeping the existing boxes—can be a smart move when the cabinet frames are square, solid, and installed correctly. In that scenario, refacing usually costs about 40–60% less than full replacement, and it limits demolition, dust, and disruption.

Refacing can also be cost-effective for homeowners who want the look of new cabinetry without waiting for a full set of new carcases. But there are limits: if your existing boxes are out of square, have water damage, or the interiors are poorly laid out, refacing won’t fix those fundamentals. Replacement becomes the better route when you need to correct nonstandard openings, add storage features, or address cabinet problems that hide behind old toe-kicks and liners.

Here’s a practical Exeter pricing example. If you’re choosing between refacing and replacement for the same footprint, refacing typically falls around $2,500–$12,000, while stock cabinet replacement is often $12,000–$45,000. If the budget is tight and the boxes are sound, spending in the refacing band is justified. If you discover missing fillers, warped rails, or significant alignment issues during tear-out, the “savings” of refacing can disappear because you’ll still pay for corrective carpentry—at which point replacement often gives a cleaner, more durable result.

In older Exeter homes, the decision often hinges on what you find during assessment: refacing is best when boxes are solid; replacement is best when your kitchen’s structure or storage plan needs real correction.

How to choose a kitchen renovation contractor in Exeter

Choosing the right kitchen renovation contractor in Exeter starts with verifying credentials and then tightening the contract details so you don’t pay for surprises. For licensing, confirm the contractor’s Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration using the online registry referenced at hic.attorneygeneral.gov for qualifying work. For electrical and plumbing scopes, verify the licensed electrician and licensed plumber separately—look them up through dli.pa.gov for electrical credentials, and request proof of PA plumbing licensure as well.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes instead of lump sums. An itemized quote should separate labor and materials for cabinets, countertops, flooring, electrical rough-ins, plumbing changes, demolition, and carpentry trim. Ask whether the price includes protective floor covering, haul-away, patch-and-paint or drywall finishing, and the cost of reconnecting sink/disposal/dishwasher plumbing. Also request any insurance claim documentation process in writing if the project relates to a prior water or fire event.

Warranty matters: ask for the length of workmanship warranty, plus separate product warranties for cabinets, countertops, and appliances. Payment terms should be conservative—never pay more than 10–15% upfront, and use a holdback until the job is complete and punch-list items are corrected. Finally, insist on a written timeline with arrival estimates, key milestones (demo, rough-ins, cabinet install, countertop template and install, flooring), and a completion window.

Should I reface or replace my kitchen cabinets in Exeter?
