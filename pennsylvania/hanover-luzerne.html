Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, kitchen renovations aren’t just a design decision—they’re a licensing and permitting decision. For contractor work over $500, Pennsylvania requires Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration for covered home improvement services (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). For any kitchen electrical changes, a licensed PA electrician must install or modify circuits (verify at dli.pa.gov). Plumbing work—such as moving a sink, adding a dishwasher hookup, or relocating plumbing—must be performed by or supervised by a PA-licensed plumber.

Permits and inspections are commonly required in most PA municipalities when you’re doing plumbing rerouting, adding new electrical circuits, or removing walls/altering structural elements. Cosmetic work—like painting, swapping hardware, and cabinet refacing without touching plumbing or electrical—typically does not require a permit. Because Hanover and Luzerne County homeowners often renovate older homes, lead paint rules also matter: for pre-1978 homes, contractors disturbing lead-based paint surfaces must be EPA-compliant. EPA Lead-Safe RRP certification is required for contractors working where lead paint may be disturbed, and you can verify certifications through the EPA Lead-Safe RRP registry (certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov) before a gut remodel.

Step-by-step for Hanover homeowners: (1) Ask for the contractor’s PA HIC number and confirm it online via the HIC registration resource; (2) request proof of general liability insurance and workers’ compensation—then verify the certificate names the correct business entity; (3) if electrical or plumbing is in scope, confirm those trades are licensed and provide their license numbers before work begins; (4) ensure the contract includes a written description of work, start dates, and change-order process; Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act also requires written contracts for home improvement work over $500, including a 3-day right of cancellation—request this in writing.

Cabinet refacing vs. replacement — what makes sense in Hanover?

With 76.8% of Hanover’s housing stock built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many homeowners are working with 30–50-year-old kitchens where the cabinet boxes may still be structurally serviceable, but the style, doors, and finish feel dated. That’s why cabinet refacing is a common middle ground in Luzerne County: you keep the existing cabinet boxes and replace only the door and drawer fronts (plus veneer and hardware as appropriate). When it fits, refacing typically costs about 40–60% less than full replacement, and it can reduce demolition time compared to a full gut.

However, refacing only makes sense when your boxes are square, solid, and in acceptable interior condition. In older Hanover homes, doors may not align anymore, face frames may be worn, and moisture issues can hide behind older finishes. If your cabinet boxes have warped rails, water damage, or nonstandard openings that require major modifications, you can end up paying for refacing and then still needing adjustments—making full replacement the smarter long-term value.

A practical dollar example: if you want updated hardware, new door styles, and a cleaner look, refacing in the $2,500–$12,000 range can be a justified choice when the box structure is straight and the layout is already functional. But if you’re also trying to correct sagging uppers, replace damaged lower bases, or upgrade to modern storage layouts, cabinet replacement in the $12,000–$45,000 band often pays off by addressing the underlying condition—not just the face.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes. Look for a line-by-line breakdown of labor versus materials: cabinets (and whether they’re stock vs semi-custom), countertop allowance and material type, flooring type and underlayment, lighting fixtures, sink and faucet installation, and electrical/plumbing rough-in allowances.
