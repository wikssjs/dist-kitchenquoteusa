Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, kitchen renovation contractors must be properly registered and licensed for the work they do. For work over $500, Pennsylvania requires Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). For electrical work, any installation or modification of circuits must be performed by a licensed PA electrician—verify licensing at dli.pa.gov. For plumbing tasks like relocating a sink, changing dishwasher hookups, or adding new lines, the work must be performed by (or under the supervision of) a PA-licensed plumber.

Permits are typically required when you change a system or the structure of the home. In most Dauphin County municipalities, these kitchen updates usually require building permits and scheduled inspections: rerouting plumbing lines, adding new electrical circuits, moving walls or removing load-bearing sections, and any work that changes how systems run behind finishes. Cosmetic changes—such as painting, swapping hardware, and cabinet refacing that does not disturb walls or plumbing—typically does not require permits.

Lead paint is a big deal in older South Hanover kitchens. If your home is pre-1978 and contractors will disturb painted surfaces, EPA Lead-Safe RRP certification is required. Verify credentials using the EPA Certified Renovator search before hiring anyone for a gut remodel in older homes (certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov).

Finally, under the PA Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, contracts for home improvement work over $500 must be written and include a 3-day right of cancellation. When hiring, South Hanover homeowners should: (1) confirm the HIC registration online, (2) request the electrician/plumber’s license details, (3) collect certificates of insurance, and (4) provide proof of workers’ compensation coverage—then verify each document matches the contractor’s legal name and the scope in the written estimate.

Cabinet refacing vs. replacement — what makes sense in South Hanover?

With roughly 35.5% of South Hanover’s housing stock built before 1980, many Dauphin County kitchens are dealing with 30–50-year-old finishes where the cabinet boxes may still be structurally usable. In those situations, cabinet refacing (new door and drawer fronts plus veneer and hardware, while keeping the existing boxes) is often the “best value” middle step. In general, refacing runs about 40–60% less than a full replacement, and it can also reduce job disruption—important for homeowners who want their kitchens back faster.

Refacing only makes financial sense when the existing boxes are square, solid, and in acceptable interior condition. If the old boxes are warped, have chronic moisture issues, or the layout is dated (wrong storage layout, cramped corner access, missing appliance spaces), replacement is usually the better route. Replacement also becomes the practical choice when you need to correct ventilation clearances, adjust for modern appliance dimensions, or redesign the workflow—often paired with countertop replacement.

Here’s where homeowners usually see the difference clearly. If your current layout is functional but the doors and finish look worn, refacing may land around $2,500–$12,000 while keeping the boxes. But if you’re planning to replace the base cabinets under the sink area because of old plumbing damage or to rework pull-outs and pantry storage, replacement (stock or semi-custom) can move into $12,000–$45,000. In older South Hanover homes, that justification is often less about aesthetics and more about solving what you can’t see until demolition begins.

How to choose a kitchen renovation contractor in South Hanover

Choosing the right kitchen renovation contractor in South Hanover starts with verifying credentials before you sign anything. Confirm the company’s Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration for qualifying work at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. For electrical scope, ask for proof that a licensed PA electrician will be performing circuit modifications—verify at dli.pa.gov. For plumbing changes, request the licensed plumber’s information and make sure the work is being performed under the appropriate license. Then, ask for insurance documents: a current certificate of insurance and proof of workers’ compensation coverage. Make sure the names on the certificates match the contractor you hire and the scope in your estimate.

Next, get 2–3 itemized, written quotes—not just a lump sum—so you can compare labor and materials line-by-line (cabinets, countertops, flooring underlayment/base work, electrical/plumbing allowances, and disposal). Read the scope for what’s excluded (for example: permits, drywall repair beyond a certain allowance, subfloor leveling, or appliance hookup). Under the PA Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, ensure your contract is written for work over $500 and clearly states the terms and cancellation rights.

Paying and scheduling should be controlled: never pay more than 10–15% upfront, and use a holdback until the job is complete and the punch list is finished. Ask for an arrival estimate and a completion window in writing, including countertop lead times and inspection scheduling if permits are required.
