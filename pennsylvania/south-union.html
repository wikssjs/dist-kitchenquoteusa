Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, kitchen renovations aren’t just a design project—they’re also a licensing and permitting project. For the work itself, Pennsylvania requires a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration for home improvement work over $500 (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). For electrical, any time circuits are installed or modified, a licensed PA electrician must perform the work—verify credentials and status through DLI resources at dli.pa.gov. For plumbing, sink/dishwasher hookup changes, or any relocation of plumbing, must be performed by or under the supervision of a PA-licensed plumber.

Permits are commonly required when the renovation involves plumbing rerouting, new electrical circuits, or wall removal. Cosmetic work typically does not require a permit, including paint, installing new hardware, updating lighting fixtures without circuit changes, and cabinet refacing where no structural, electrical, or plumbing changes are made.

Because many South Union homes were built before 1978, lead paint may be present on kitchen surfaces. If the project disturbs lead-painted surfaces, contractors performing that work generally need EPA Lead-Safe RRP certification. Before hiring for a gut remodel in older South Union homes, verify certification at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov.

Under the PA Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, a written contract is required for projects over $500, including the 3-day right of cancellation. When you’re vetting a contractor, confirm the HIC number online, review the certificate of insurance (general liability) and request proof of workers’ compensation coverage, and ensure the correct licensed trades will be performing plumbing and electrical. Then build your timeline with the understanding that permit review and inspections can add time before we start demo.

Cabinet refacing vs. replacement — what makes sense in South Union?

In South Union, the “right” cabinet plan often depends on whether the cabinet boxes are still structurally sound. With about 70% of the housing stock built before 1980, many Fayette County homeowners inherit kitchens with dated door styles but cabinets that can remain serviceable if they haven’t warped, separated from the walls, or been plagued by water intrusion.

Cabinet refacing—replacing only the door/drawer fronts and veneer while keeping the existing box structure—is popular because it typically costs 40–60% less than full replacement. In practical terms, it can be a smart middle ground when your boxes are square, firmly attached, and in acceptable interior condition. It also helps preserve a known layout when you don’t want the disruptions of a full teardown.

However, refacing isn’t a fit when the existing boxes are out of level, have failing particleboard frames, or won’t line up correctly with new doors. If the layout is functional but storage is poor, refacing may not solve the problem because interior storage details typically remain the same. In those cases, cabinet replacement (stock or semi-custom) gives you the ability to correct plan issues—like adding pull-outs, adjusting for appliance clearance, or changing toe-kick dimensions for better accessibility.

For a dollar-level example, consider a homeowner choosing between refacing (often in the $3,200–$9,000 range) and full replacement (commonly $9,000–$42,000). If the boxes are solid and you only need a modern look, the refacing savings are justified. If the boxes are sagging or water-damaged behind lowers, spending toward replacement can be the more durable investment—and it helps prevent repeat labor later.

How to choose a kitchen renovation contractor in South Union

Choosing the right contractor is what keeps a South Union kitchen project on schedule and on budget—especially for older homes that often require electrical and plumbing corrections before finishes go in. Start by verifying IICRC certification (if applicable to the work being offered) and the required Pennsylvania licensing, then check each item directly in online registries. For Pennsylvania contractor requirements, use the HIC registration lookup via hic.attorneygeneral.gov and confirm licensed trade credentials through DLI-related resources (for electricians and plumbing supervision). For liability coverage, request certificates of insurance and confirm that workers’ compensation is in place before work begins.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes. Ask for a breakdown that separates labor and materials—cabinets, countertops, flooring prep, plumbing fixtures, electrical work, drywall/paint, demolition, disposal, and any permit-related costs—rather than a single lump sum. Carefully read what’s excluded: for example, any allowance for subfloor repairs, hidden framing repairs, or electrical code corrections. Also ask whether the contractor documents insurance-claim scenarios properly if you’re using an HOA or insurance process.

Warranty is another deciding factor. Look for a workmanship warranty length and confirm what’s covered for cabinets, countertops, flooring installation, and appliance hookups. For payment, use a schedule that never requires more than about 10–15% upfront, then hold back a portion until the job is complete and cleaned. Finally, insist on a written arrival estimate and completion window, because lead times for cabinet-heavy work can be longer, particularly in spring and fall.

Should I reface or replace my kitchen cabinets in South Union?

In South Union, the most cost-effective choice usually depends on the condition of your existing cabinet boxes, not just how dated the doors look. Because many homes here were built before 1980, it’s common to find cabinets that still hold up structurally, even when the finish style is worn. If your boxes are square, firmly attached, and interiors are in good shape, refacing is often the better move—typically in the $3,200–$9,000 range—because you keep the layout and reduce demolition and rework. If the boxes are water-damaged, pulled away from the wall, or out of level (which can show up only after floors are evaluated), replacement tends to be the safer long-term solution, usually starting around the $9,000–$42,000 band depending on whether you choose stock or semi-custom. Replacing is also the right call if you need storage upgrades that require internal changes, like different drawer configurations or pull-out systems. We recommend a site walkthrough where we check box squareness, attachment to framing, and interior condition before you decide.

What countertop is best for a South Union kitchen?

Granite is a strong midrange choice if you like natural stone variation and want a more unique look.
