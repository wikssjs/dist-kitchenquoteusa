Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

For North Londonderry homeowners, the fastest way to avoid delays is to plan for permits and licensing early—especially in kitchens that involve plumbing and electrical. In Pennsylvania, contractors performing home improvement work may need a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration when the job is over $500 (verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov). If your renovation involves kitchen electrical—new circuits, moving outlets, upgrading to serve a range, or any modification to how circuits are installed—work must be performed by or supervised by a licensed Pennsylvania electrician (confirm licensing at dli.pa.gov). Plumbing changes—such as relocating a sink, modifying a dishwasher hookup, or rerouting drain lines—must be performed by or under the supervision of a PA-licensed plumber.

Permits are often required in most PA municipalities when you do plumbing rerouting, add new electrical circuits, or remove/alter walls. Cosmetic-only work—painting, replacing hardware, or cabinet refacing—typically doesn’t require a building permit. However, gut remodels in older North Londonderry homes may involve lead-based paint risk. If the home is pre-1978, lead paint may be present on kitchen surfaces; contractors disturbing that paint generally need EPA Lead-Safe RRP certification. Before hiring, verify at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov.

Finally, Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires written contracts for home improvement work over $500, including a 3-day right of cancellation. Ask for the contract in writing and review the exact scope.

Step-by-step verification: (1) confirm HIC registration (if applicable) via hic.attorneygeneral.gov, (2) confirm the electrician and plumber are properly licensed via dli.pa.gov, (3) request certificates of insurance showing general liability and workers’ compensation coverage, and (4) ensure your contractor’s proposal matches those trades. A reputable contractor will hand you proof of insurance and workers’ comp before work starts—no chasing required.

Cabinet refacing vs. replacement — what makes sense in North Londonderry?

With about 47% of North Londonderry’s housing stock built before 1980, many Lebanon County homeowners inherit kitchens where the cabinet boxes are still solid but the face style, finish, and hardware look dated. Cabinet refacing—keeping the existing cabinet boxes and replacing only the door and drawer fronts (and applying veneer to the exposed surfaces)—is often the middle path. In Pennsylvania kitchen projects, refacing commonly costs 40–60% less than full replacement when the boxes are square, secure, and in good interior condition.

In practice, refacing is most sensible when you’re targeting a visible refresh: a new shaker door style, updated hinges, and a more modern finish, without needing to re-engineer storage. It is usually cost-effective when the box frames are stable, the doors close properly, and there’s no water damage, delamination, or out-of-level installation that would telegraph through the new veneer. If your existing cabinets are warped, pulled away from the wall, or have moisture damage behind them, refacing won’t “fix” the structural problem—you’ll end up paying for a cosmetic layer over a foundation that still needs correction.

Here’s a concrete example from North Londonderry-style projects: if you can keep well-built stock boxes and just change the look, refacing may land around the $2,500–$8,000 band. But if inspection during demo shows the boxes are out of square and the layout needs better drawers, organizers, or a sink base relocated for improved workflow, cabinet replacement can be the better value—even though it may push closer to $8,000–$35,000 for stock/semi-custom. In older homes, that “difference” is often justified by functionality and time saved on rework.

To choose correctly, start with an on-site assessment of the box condition, door alignment, and water exposure history. Then decide whether you need only a face lift—or if you truly need new boxes to match modern kitchen performance and storage standards.
