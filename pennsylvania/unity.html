Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, permits and licensed-trade requirements aren’t optional when kitchen work affects safety systems. For home improvement work over $500, Pennsylvania requires kitchen renovation contractors to hold Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). For electrical, any circuit installation or modification in a kitchen must be performed by a licensed PA electrician (verify at dli.pa.gov). For plumbing—sink hookups, dishwasher connections, or any relocation—work must be performed by or under the supervision of a PA-licensed plumber.

Permits are typically required when the project involves plumbing rerouting, new electrical circuits, or wall removal/structural changes. Cosmetic work—like paint, hardware changes, and cabinet refacing where you’re not changing electrical/plumbing—often does not require permits, but it still needs to be handled by properly licensed trades when trades are involved.

Lead paint is a big consideration in Unity. Many homes were built before 1978, and lead-based paint can exist on kitchen surfaces. If your renovation disturbs lead paint, contractors must have EPA Lead-Safe RRP certification (verify at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov before hiring for a gut remodel in older Unity homes).

Finally, the Pennsylvania Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires written contracts for home improvement work over $500, including a 3-day right of cancellation. When you hire, confirm your contractor’s credentials and protection: request a copy of HIC registration, verify electrician/plumber licensing, and ask for certificates of insurance and proof of workers’ compensation coverage. Keep those documents with your signed contract before any demo starts.

Cabinet refacing vs. replacement — what makes sense in Unity?

With roughly 60.8% of Unity’s housing stock built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many kitchens are “structurally fine but stylistically tired.” That’s why cabinet refacing is a popular middle ground in Westmoreland County: you keep the existing cabinet boxes and update only the door/drawer fronts and veneer. When done correctly, refacing typically costs 40–60% less than full replacement because you’re not removing and resetting every box and you usually avoid a lot of wall patching.

However, refacing only makes sense when the existing cabinet boxes are square, solid, and in acceptable interior condition. In older Unity homes, some boxes show water damage near sinks, loose fasteners around older plumbing leaks, or warping from years of steam and humidity—those conditions can make refacing look uneven or fail sooner. If your boxes aren’t true, replacement may actually be the better value.

Here’s a concrete example: say you’re considering quartz counters plus a refresh of your kitchen appearance. If your cabinets are solid, refacing can land around $2,500–$8,000, freeing budget for better countertops and new lighting. But if your cabinet run is out of square and you need multiple fillers just to close gaps, replacement (often $8,000–$35,000 for stock/semi-custom) can prevent an ongoing “racking” look and reduce the risk of future alignment issues when the counters are installed.

How to choose a kitchen renovation contractor in Unity

Choosing the right contractor in Unity is mostly about verification and clarity. Start with credentials: confirm the contractor’s IICRC certification and Pennsylvania licensing by using online registries (then cross-check with the person who will actually supervise your project). When it’s time to request a bid, ask for 2–3 itemized written quotes that break labor and materials out separately—don’t accept a single lump sum if you’re comparing options. A good quote will clearly list demolition, framing/patching, cabinet installation, countertop fabrication/templating, electrical and plumbing scopes (with licensed trades noted), flooring prep, and appliance install responsibilities.

Read the scope line-by-line for what’s excluded. Many conflicts come from “allowances” that aren’t defined: lighting fixtures, disposal installation, venting assumptions, or whether drywall/painting is included after cabinet work. Also ask whether insurance claim documentation is included if your project is related to damage—kitchen renovations after leaks are common in older Westmoreland homes.

Warranty should be in writing: a workmanship warranty length, plus manufacturer warranties for cabinets, countertops, and installed equipment. For payment schedule, aim for a structure that never puts you at risk: limit upfront payment to about 10–15%, and hold back until key milestones are finished and verified. Finally, get the arrival estimate and completion window in writing, including how long ordering items (cabinets and countertops) will take—this matters in 2026 when full-gut work can book out 6–12 weeks, longer for layout changes.
