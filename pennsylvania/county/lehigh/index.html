What a kitchen remodel costs in Lehigh County

Lehigh County homeowners, with a median household income of $90,161 and more than 83,000 owner-occupied households (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), are often at the decision point of whether now is the moment for a kitchen remodel. For most, the starting point is shaped by both their home’s vintage and the available budget. Many tract homes in Upper and Lower Macungie, built during the post-1960 suburban wave, are now entering the age where a full kitchen replacement is often more practical than another patch job. In these homes, a 1990s builder-grade kitchen may just need fresh cabinetry and finishes. But for anyone with an older 1950s or 1960s galley kitchen, opening up the space can become a major structural and mechanical project.

The single most common source of cost overruns in Lehigh County kitchens is specifying one type of project — a straight cabinet swap — and then upgrading to relocated plumbing, structural changes, or high-end finishes midstream. To avoid surprise, decide early whether you are keeping the layout or moving walls and services. Even changing cabinet grade can put you into a new cost class. Here’s how the price bands break out for typical projects in this market:

Layout changes and the cost cliff

The “cost cliff” in Lehigh County kitchen remodeling is no exaggeration: keeping the sink, range, and fridge in place means you’re working within a known, contained budget. But move just one of those — especially across the room — and costs jump sharply. Plumbing changes require both slope for drain lines and sometimes running new lines beneath a slab or through finished ceilings, neither of which is trivial. There’s a real engineering hurdle to gaining even a few feet of distance, especially in older homes common across the county.

Electrical upgrades to accommodate a relocated range or added circuits touch your home’s panel, and many kitchens in Lehigh County’s post-1960s tract homes have panels that are at or near capacity. Moving a wall, particularly if it may be load-bearing (not uncommon in those Macungie subdivisions), means paying for a structural engineer and plan review. Every one of these steps also triggers the need for municipal permits and inspections — in Allentown, that includes plan review before issuance for any mechanical, layout, or system change.

Keep this in mind: a gut renovation that leaves the layout unchanged can be done for $31,500–$126,000. Move plumbing and electrical, and you’re instantly at $68,500–$126,000. That jump is not proportional to how far things move — the first foot costs as much as the fourth. If the current layout is simply uninspiring but functional, often the better use of funds is higher-quality cabinetry or better appliances, not chasing a new floor plan.

What requires a permit in a kitchen renovation

In Lehigh County, the threshold for triggering a building permit is clear: cosmetic work like painting, cabinet refacing, and even countertop replacement (as long as plumbing and electrical stay put) often require no permit at all, especially in the surrounding townships as well as Allentown. But the moment you plan to move plumbing, install new electrical circuits, or relocate gas lines, a permit is definitely required within Allentown city limits, and almost always in other municipalities.

For projects in Allentown that involve layout changes, new mechanicals, or structural work, you should expect a city plan review before any permits are issued. In townships, the process is similar or handled by their own code officials. Electrical inspections are done by Allentown’s code office, but in nearby townships third-party agencies often handle the inspections, surprising many first-time renovators who expect only township staff.

Contractors must hold current Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, issued by the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General. You can verify this at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. If the contractor has employees, they must carry workers' compensation insurance; check status through the state-run WCAIS system at wcais.pa.gov. Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires a written contract for any job over $500 and gives you three business days to cancel — make sure your contract contains this language.

Ignoring permits can haunt you at resale, especially in high-turnover areas like the West End of Allentown, where unpermitted kitchen work is seen as a red flag in closings and appraisals.

Appliance choices and the decisions they force

Appliance decisions aren’t the last step in a Lehigh County kitchen project — they’re the first. Cabinets are built to appliance specs. Ordering cabinets before finalizing appliance models is how people end up with a refrigerator that juts out or a range that doesn’t fit. Standard refrigerators will often protrude beyond standard cabinetry; counter-depth models solve this but cut capacity and cost more, and their width/door clearance may push you toward semi-custom cabinets.

Range decisions matter plenty. Slide-in ranges need cabinetry trimmed to fit, but look more integrated. Freestanding ranges work with most stock cabinets. Gas or induction both require checking the existing wiring and gas supply. Induction ranges need a strong 240V circuit, not often present in pre-1980 homes in Lehigh County’s older stock. Panel-ready appliances (especially dishwashers) require advance measuring and precise cabinetry ordering.

Range hood choices — ducted versus recirculating — are not just aesthetics: ducted vents out an exterior wall, which may be impossible or expensive to add after cabinetry is in. Recirculating hoods are the compromise when routing ductwork isn't feasible, but they just don’t clear cooking odors and moisture as well.

On “pro” appliances: the uplift is mostly in build quality and resale optics in premium neighborhoods — not in real cooking performance that most families use. Also, appliance lead times on some desirable models now outstrip cabinet timelines, so never leave choices to the end.

Four kinds of kitchen project, four budgets

In Lehigh County, “kitchen remodel” can mean anything from a long weekend of new paint to a full gut job with walls moved and mechanicals relocated. The cost range runs fivefold or more. Here's how the typical jobs break down:

  • Cosmetic refresh: Paint, upgraded hardware, maybe a new backsplash, minor lighting improvements. No cabinets or layout changes. Ideal if you're happy with the current layout but want a fresh look. Budget for mostly DIY or weekend handymen, rather than contractors.
  • Pull and replace: Cabinets and countertops come out and go back into the exact same layout. Existing plumbing and electrical stay put. All labor and finish upgrades, but no wall or floor plan changes.
  • Gut, same layout: Everything down to the studs, all surfaces and systems replaced, but fixtures, sink, dishwasher, and range all stay where they are — no new drain or circuit runs. A common scenario for Lehigh County tract homes built between 1960 and 2000.
  • Layout change: Walls move, plumbing, gas, or electrical lines are re-routed. This is where engineering, plan review, and new permits kick in, especially in Allentown.

The price cliff sits right between gutting in place and moving things. At $31,500–$126,000, you can replace most everything if it stays roughly where it is. Shift the sink or stove, and your minimum jumps dramatically, mostly for engineering, permits, and specialty trades. The best step before calling contractors is to decide your real scope tier—for both budget and timing. Until you do, no reputable contractor in Lehigh County can give you a number that means anything.

Cabinets: reface, stock, semi-custom or custom

Cabinet decisions drive both look and budget. There are four main routes:

  • Refacing means keeping existing box frames and replacing only the doors, drawer fronts, and applying a wood or laminate veneer. It’s a good solution when cabinet boxes are well-built, still square, and the layout remains functional. Refacing cannot create more storage or fix a bad layout, but in many Lehigh County tract kitchens from the ‘80s and ‘90s, it’s a straight path to a fresh look for far less than a full gut.
  • Stock cabinets are pre-sized and best for rooms with standard wall dimensions. They’re quick and cost-effective, but filler panels become inevitable in older homes or any space not perfectly square — which is common here.
  • Semi-custom cabinets allow changes in width, depth, and finishes. Most Lehigh County jobs tilt this direction because suburban houses, especially post-1960s, often have settling or non-standard wall lengths that stock cabinets just can’t match neatly.
  • Full custom should honestly be reserved for unique layouts, luxury finishes, or personal design statements where nothing off the shelf fits — or satisfies you.

Construction quality isn’t all about price: Plywood boxes last longest, while particleboard is cheaper and less durable. Dovetail drawers and full-extension soft-close hardware add lifespan and convenience. Frameless (European-style) boxes provide more accessible storage, while face-frame cabinets are the American standard. The reality in Lehigh County is that settling walls and decades of small remodels push more projects toward semi-custom whether or not the owner began with that intention.

Where the money actually goes

Seeing the breakdown of a kitchen budget in real dollars can clarify what matters. In Lehigh County, out of a typical $68,500 gut remodel, more than a quarter will go to cabinetry alone — the largest and slowest-moving line on most projects. Labor and installation is the next biggest chunk, taking 20–30% and climbing if the layout changes or specialty trades are needed. Countertops, appliances, flooring, and fixtures each occupy smaller shares, but tend to dominate conversations more than their dollar share justifies.

Many homeowners overlook two crucial lines: contingency and soft costs (permits, design, delivery, and disposal). In homes predating 1980, hidden issues are so common that carrying a 10–15% contingency is mandatory, not optional.

Where to invest an extra thousand? Upgrading from particleboard to plywood cabinets, or from a standard to a quartz countertop, ages better than splurging on a commercial-grade cooktop when the rest of the kitchen is builder-basic. High-end appliances in a low-end setting rarely return value. Every percentage point below is based on Lehigh County cost bands, so you can estimate the real range for your project.

Eight weeks without a kitchen

For many in Lehigh County, the hardest part of a kitchen renovation isn’t the noise, or even the outflow of money — it’s losing the use of your kitchen for up to eight weeks, sometimes longer if the scope changes or materials run late. Setting up survival mode matters. Many homeowners move a fridge and a microwave to the dining room, use a folding table as the prep counter, and wash up in a laundry tub. Expect to rely on a coffee maker and slow cooker more than you ever did before. Stock up on paper plates and plan for more takeout — two working adults and kids eating out several times a week can run higher than your new flooring. Allentown permits usually require dumpsters on site, so block off part of your driveway or lawn for the crew’s use.

Dust control should not be a best-effort, especially in older homes with open returns and hardwood floors. Plastic sheeting with zip-wall style barriers contain the mess, but you’ll still be cleaning post-construction. Be upfront with your contractor about pets and access — workers will come and go all day, doors aren’t always kept closed, and some homes see drafts all day for weeks. Demolition week is the hardest; if you have tight schedules or allergies, consider moving out for that stretch if possible. Plan for real life to feel messier and more expensive while the work is happening.

Frequently asked questions — kitchen renovation in Lehigh County

Can I renovate a kitchen in stages? Staged kitchen renovation is possible in Lehigh County and sometimes a smart path for spreading out costs or avoiding a long period without a kitchen. You might do cabinets and counters this year, appliances or flooring next. But there are tradeoffs: you can’t avoid dust and disruption twice, and some scopes, like quartz countertops, can make later cabinet or sink replacement more complicated and expensive. Most Lehigh County contractors will break projects into phases if you request it, but there can be added labor costs for returning multiple times. If you plan to do the full gut over a couple of years, budget for at least $31,500–$126,000 eventually, because separate phases often nudge the total higher than a single project.
What flooring works best in a kitchen? In Lehigh County’s climate and house stock, resilient flooring — LVP (luxury vinyl plank), tile, and site-finished hardwood — are popular picks for kitchens. Tile is unbeatable for moisture and durability but colder underfoot in a climate with 6,100 heating degree days. LVP offers excellent water resistance for a lower installed cost and has taken much of the mid-market. Be cautious with floating laminate in older houses where uneven floors or direct sunlight can cause gaps and buckling. Expect a kitchen flooring job to fall between $5,300–$21,000 depending on material and prep. Consider what’s under your current floor before deciding — two decades-old sheet vinyl atop original plank can eat half the budget in tear-out and leveling.
How should renovation payments be scheduled? Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act dictates written contracts and installment schedules for jobs over $500. In Lehigh County, reputable contractors will never ask for more than a third of the total as an initial deposit. Typical draws are: 10–30% at contract signing for materials, another third at halfway (often after cabinet delivery or rough inspections), and the balance due at completion. Never pay in full until the job’s inspected and you’re satisfied. If your total renovation is $68,500, expect about $6,800–$20,500 upfront before work begins. Never pay in cash; checks and electronic payments leave an audit trail if disputes arise.
What adds the most resale value in a kitchen? In Lehigh County’s current market, modern cabinets and well-chosen countertops add the most value at resale — they’re what buyers see on entry. Appliances help if they’re current and match, but high-end brands won’t push your price higher than a fresh, functional layout. Bringing in light (under-cabinet, pendants) is particularly appealing in West End Allentown and similar neighborhoods. Full gut remodels can reach $31,500–$126,000 but not all homes can support a top-of-market investment — keep spending in line with surrounding values. Avoid over-investing in pro-style ranges or imported tile in modest homes; quality, practical upgrades look best to most buyers at closing.
How do I verify a kitchen contractor in Pennsylvania? Verify any Lehigh County kitchen contractor’s Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov, maintained by the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General. For jobs over $500, HICPA requires a written contract with their registration number on it, and gives you a three-day right to cancel. If the contractor has employees, check their workers' compensation status at wcais.pa.gov. Never work with anyone who balks at these checks. For gut remodels and major changes, check references and pay attention to how they handle permits — a reputable contractor won’t suggest skipping them, especially not for jobs in the $31,500–$126,000 range.
Can I live in the house during a kitchen renovation? Most Lehigh County homeowners do live at home during kitchen renovations, setting up a temporary kitchen in another room. It’s workable, but expect dust, noise, and tradespeople coming and going for 6–10 weeks. For families with pets or young kids, even a well-run job can disrupt routines. The week of demolition is hardest, so consider staying elsewhere during that period if possible. Food costs rise, sometimes by as much as the new floor — a two-month stretch can rival the $5,300–$21,000 spend on flooring. Honest conversations with the contractor about working hours and cleanup routines help everyone.
Is lead paint a concern in Lehigh County kitchen renovations? Yes, especially in homes built before 1978 — which is most houses in the county’s older urban neighborhoods. The federal EPA Lead RRP Rule governs disturbance of painted surfaces in these homes, and Lehigh County kitchens are no exception. If your house predates 1980, only hire contractors certified in lead-safe renovation (verify at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov). Expect some cost premium, but containment and cleanup are not where to cut corners — disturbing lead paint can put family and workers at risk. Even simple kitchen refacing can require extra precautions and testing, sometimes adding $1,000 or more to jobs in the $7,400–$21,000 range.
How much more does moving the sink and range cost? In Lehigh County, the single priciest upgrade you can make to a kitchen plan is moving the sink or range. Even shifts of a few feet mean running new supply, drain, gas, or 240-volt electrical lines — which triggers plan review, permits, and often upgrading panels or cutting open finished ceilings. Realistically, moving plumbing and electrical bumps you from a gut-within-the-layout spend of $31,500–$126,000 up to at least $68,500–$126,000. Most of this jump is about trades, not materials. Before redrawing, weigh whether the new flow justifies the doubled cost.
Should I order cabinets before demolition? You shouldn’t order cabinets until the demolition reveals what’s behind the old boxes, especially in Lehigh County homes built before 1980. Walls are rarely perfectly square, and plumbing surprises are the rule, not the exception. A good approach, especially on jobs costing $7,400–$31,500, is to demolish and measure bare walls before placing the cabinet order — this makes semi-custom tweaking affordable insurance. Your timeline will stretch (lead times of 6–12 weeks aren’t unusual), but you’ll avoid the even costlier mistake of cabinets not fitting. Plan your sequence for accuracy, not for speed.
Does replacing cabinets require a permit? In most Lehigh County municipalities, including Allentown, simply swapping cabinets in and out (with no layout, plumbing, or electrical changes) does not require a permit. The same goes for refacing and repainting. But as soon as you plan to move plumbing lines, install new electrical circuits, or add gas, a permit is required — and triggering that brings plan review and inspections. Even when a job looks "like for like," check with your township code office before starting. Major jobs in the $7,400–$31,500 range can damage resale value if work is unpermitted or cannot be documented later.
How much does a kitchen renovation cost in Lehigh County? Kitchen renovation pricing in Lehigh County runs a wide range. A straightforward cabinet swap with mid-grade counters can cost between $7,400–$31,500 depending on selections and complexity. Replace just the countertops, and budget $4,200–$17,000. A full gut, keeping the footprint the same, runs from $31,500–$126,000, with most households landing in the $50,000–$80,000 range. Moving major plumbing, gas, or walls moves you into the higher band, potentially up to $126,000 for full layout changes. These figures reflect typical Lehigh County labor costs and material selections, not best-case DIY numbers.
